review_text: To round out your Foley tour, a visit to the Holmes Medical Museum is an experience unlike any other.

It is housed in what was Baldwin County’s very first hospital (operating from 1936 to 1958), and the level of preservation is truly startling. It doesn't feel like a modern building filled with old things; it feels like the doctors and nurses just stepped out for a lunch break and never came back.

Authenticity and Artifacts
The museum is a "time capsule" in the truest sense. Walking through the original operating rooms and wards, you see an incredible array of authentic artifacts that are both fascinating and, at times, a bit chilling:

The "Torture" Tools: You’ll see vintage surgical instruments, heavy iron bone saws, and early X-ray machines that look more like science fiction props than medical tools.

The Iron Lung: One of the most sobering sights is the massive iron lung, a powerful reminder of the polio era and how much medicine has evolved.

Quackery Displays: They even have a section dedicated to "medical quackery"—strange devices from the past that were sold as miracle cures but were actually just elaborate hoaxes.

Exceptional Guidance
Just like at the Railroad Museum, the lady at the front desk was the heart of the experience. Her knowledge of the Holmes family and the history of the "Holmes Babies" (the hundreds of locals born within these specific walls) was impressive. She provided context that made the cold steel instruments feel like part of a living human story, explaining how the hospital served the community during a time when medical resources were scarce.

A Note for the Paranormal Curious
For those who are sensitive to the paranormal, the Holmes Medical Museum has a reputation that precedes it. There is a distinct shift in energy as you explore the second floor.
Many visitors and paranormal investigators report a sudden "heaviness" in the air, particularly near the old surgical ward and the nursery. Whether it's the weight of the history or something more "active," the atmosphere is undeniable. It’s a must-feel for anyone who enjoys a bit of a thrill alongside their history lesson.

reviewer_name: Randall Deaton
rating: 5
body: To round out your Foley tour, a visit to the Holmes Medical Museum is an experience unlike any other.

It is housed in what was Baldwin County’s very first hospital (operating from 1936 to 1958), and the level of preservation is truly startling. It doesn't feel like a modern building filled with old things; it feels like the doctors and nurses just stepped out for a lunch break and never came back.

Authenticity and Artifacts
The museum is a "time capsule" in the truest sense. Walking through the original operating rooms and wards, you see an incredible array of authentic artifacts that are both fascinating and, at times, a bit chilling:

The "Torture" Tools: You’ll see vintage surgical instruments, heavy iron bone saws, and early X-ray machines that look more like science fiction props than medical tools.

The Iron Lung: One of the most sobering sights is the massive iron lung, a powerful reminder of the polio era and how much medicine has evolved.

Quackery Displays: They even have a section dedicated to "medical quackery"—strange devices from the past that were sold as miracle cures but were actually just elaborate hoaxes.

Exceptional Guidance
Just like at the Railroad Museum, the lady at the front desk was the heart of the experience. Her knowledge of the Holmes family and the history of the "Holmes Babies" (the hundreds of locals born within these specific walls) was impressive. She provided context that made the cold steel instruments feel like part of a living human story, explaining how the hospital served the community during a time when medical resources were scarce.

A Note for the Paranormal Curious
For those who are sensitive to the paranormal, the Holmes Medical Museum has a reputation that precedes it. There is a distinct shift in energy as you explore the second floor.
Many visitors and paranormal investigators report a sudden "heaviness" in the air, particularly near the old surgical ward and the nursery. Whether it's the weight of the history or something more "active," the atmosphere is undeniable. It’s a must-feel for anyone who enjoys a bit of a thrill alongside their history lesson.
