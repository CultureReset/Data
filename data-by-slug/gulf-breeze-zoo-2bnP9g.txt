body: This was a tough visit for me. In principle, I don’t agree with traditional zoos, but my family was visiting and really wanted to go. That said, this is a well-maintained, mostly modern park, and there were several genuinely positive aspects.

My highlights were the feeding areas for the domesticated farm animals—goats, pigs, and ducks—and the 30-acre preserve with elevated walkway, which offers a more African safari–style experience. In this area, animals live in a relatively natural, mixed-species habitat, including rhinos, hippos, gazelles, and orangutans. A train ride runs throughout the preserve; the line was long when we visited, but understandably so.

There are also a number of older, more traditional caged exhibits, which I personally find quite depressing. That said, most enclosures appear appropriately sized and well cared for. There’s also an aviary with budgies and chickens where visitors can feed and interact with the birds, which many people seemed to really enjoy.

By far the biggest negative of the visit was the lions, and this genuinely upset me. Their enclosure is a generic, rectangular chain-link pen that felt very small, with little to no visible enrichment (we only saw a single soccer ball). Seeing two big cats in that setting was deeply sad and, for me, overshadowed much of what the zoo otherwise does well.

I hope there are plans to expand the safari area and find a way to incorporate the lions—along with the giraffes, and perhaps camels—into a more natural, open environment. It would be wonderful to see these animals roaming in a setting that better reflects how they’re meant to live, wild and free.
