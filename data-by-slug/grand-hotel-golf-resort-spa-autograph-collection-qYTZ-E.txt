body: I don’t write negative reviews. It’s simply not my nature to try and hurt a business, especially one with as much history and meaning to a community as the Grand Hotel. That said, this is a venerable institution in these parts, and it deserves better service than what we experienced.
The Grand Hotel is now part of a larger corporate entity, and unfortunately you can feel that shift. There’s a noticeable lack of ownership and pride from the staff. This was not a five-star experience. There was no one to help with bags, no guidance to our room, and very little staff presence overall to help orient guests or make you feel truly welcomed on such a large property.
One moment in particular stood out. We arrived at one of the restaurants about 15 minutes before closing, fully intending to order a quick dessert and be mindful of the staff since they were already preparing for breakfast. Before we could even explain that, we heard a waitress audibly groan about another customer coming in so close to closing time. It made us feel intrusive and unwelcome — not a feeling you expect at a resort of this caliber.
The hotel offers Christmas activities for children, which is a great idea in theory. However, the candy cane hunt felt hastily thrown together, lacking both organization and enthusiasm from the staff — almost as if it had been planned at the last minute. In contrast, the paid holiday events open to outside guests, like the gingerbread house activity and the Christmas stroll, were very well done and genuinely enjoyable.
The Grand Hotel has an incredible legacy and reputation, and that’s exactly why this experience was disappointing. The bones of something special are still there, but the level of service and guest engagement needs to match the history and expectations that come with the name. I hope ownership takes this feedback seriously, because this place deserves to feel special again.
